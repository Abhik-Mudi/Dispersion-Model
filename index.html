01 — Overview

Two Release Models, One Framework

The module supports two canonical Gaussian atmospheric dispersion scenarios, each evaluated at any point in space and instant in time, with ground reflection via the mirror-image source term.

🌫️

Continuous Plume Release

Steady-state release rate Qm (kg/s) from an elevated source. A steady Gaussian plume is advected downwind, gated by an advection wavefront: concentration is zero until the plume front x = u·t arrives at the receptor.

PLUME MODEL
💨

Instantaneous Puff Release

Fixed total mass Qm* (kg) released at once, forming a transient Gaussian puff whose center travels with the wind at xcenter = u·t while the cloud grows in all three directions.

PUFF MODEL
📐

Stability Classes A–F

Pasquill–Gifford correlations parameterize plume spread for conditions from strongly unstable (A) to strongly stable (F).

6 CLASSES
🏙️

Rural & Urban Terrain

Separate coefficient sets account for enhanced mechanical and thermal turbulence over built-up areas.

02 — Governing Equations

The Gaussian Solutions

Both models include ground reflection by superposing an image source at −Hr, so near-ground concentrations are effectively doubled.

Plume — steady concentration

C(x, y, z) = Qm 2π σy σz u exp( −y²2σy² ) · [ exp(−(z − Hr)²2σz²) + exp(−(z + Hr)²2σz²) ]

Active only where the advection front x = u·t has passed; otherwise C = 0.

Puff — transient concentration

C(x, y, z, t) = Qm* (2π)3/2 σx σy σz exp( −(x − u·t)²2σx² ) · exp( −y²2σy² ) · [ exp(−(z − Hr)²2σz²) + exp(−(z + Hr)²2σz²) ]

σx = σy — isotropic horizontal turbulence assumption.

Unit Conversions

kg/m³ → mg/m³: ×10⁶
kg/m³ → ppm: × (24.45 × 10⁶ / MW)
where MW is molecular weight (g/mol) and 24.45 L/mol is the molar volume at 25 °C, 1 atm.

Advection Front

Plume front position: xfront = u · t
Expected arrival time: tarrival = x / u
Puff center: xcenter = u · t

Sigma Floor

Downwind distance is clamped to a minimum of 0.1 m (np.maximum(x, 0.1)) to prevent singularity at the source.

03 — Dispersion Coefficients

Plume coefficients are power-law forms with finite-distance corrections; puff coefficients follow Slade (1968) quasi-instantaneous correlations σ = a·xb.
